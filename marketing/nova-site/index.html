What it does

Not a remote-control car.
A curious little being.

Every 20 seconds NOVA looks through its camera, measures the space ahead, listens to you, then decides what to do next: speak, explore, change its mood, or use its arm. Nobody scripted it.

It sees and understands

A webcam behind the gloss visor streams to Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Google's newest Flash-tier multimodal model (switch on "ASI" mode for a bigger reasoning model). NOVA recognises objects, people and rooms, and keeps a 2D map of everything it has explored.

It talks back

Natural voice in English, Russian, German, Italian and Czech. It records your answer, keeps listening while you talk, and stops when you pause.

It remembers

Two kinds of memory. Long-term memory holds facts that survive reboots: your name, where the kitchen is. Short-term memory covers what just happened, so NOVA changes plan instead of repeating a mistake.

It's careful

Safety isn't left to the model. The controller trims every forward move to the free space the ultrasonic eyes measure, and folds the arm before driving.

You can watch live

A web dashboard shows the live camera, telemetry and the robot's thoughts. You can drive it by hand, set it a goal, or just read the diary it writes for itself every day.

Mood ring

You'll know how it feels before it says a word.

The language model picks the colour every cycle. Tap a mood to see it.

Arm lab

An arm that lives in the roof.

When NOVA drives, the two-joint arm folds flat into a bay in the roof. When it unfolds, it reaches the floor just in front of the bumper, right where the camera is looking. Move the joints yourself. These are the same angles the firmware uses.

Built-in skills

The model chooses from 14 skills, including tap, flick, scoop, push and wave. Every move is eased on the microcontroller, and checked with forward kinematics so the claw never hits the body or the floor.

How it thinks

A consciousness loop, every 20 seconds.

Sense

Camera frame, ultrasonic distance, temperature, humidity, and your voice.

Recall

Goal, plan, map, long-term memory and the last 12 events.

Reason

Gemini 4 Hydrogen thinks it over and returns structured JSON.

Act

Drive, turn, run an arm skill, speak, play a sound, change mood.

Reflect

Write the outcome to memory. Change strategy if something keeps failing.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Do I need a 3D printer?

No. Every part comes printed in its final colour. The STL files are included too, so if you have a printer you can reprint, repair or remix anything.

Is it really autonomous?

Yes. With AGI mode on, NOVA runs its own sense, think, act loop. It decides where to go, what to say and what to do with its arm, all toward a goal you give it in plain language. You can take manual control from the dashboard at any moment.

Can the arm pick things up?

The arm has two joints and a fixed hook-claw. It can touch, tap, flick, push, scoop and hook light objects, but it doesn't grip. The design leaves room to upgrade to a gripper later.

How hard is the assembly?

If you can use a screwdriver and plug in jumper wires, you can build NOVA. There's no soldering. Plan on about an hour, following the illustrated guide.

What are the running costs?

NOVA thinks with Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen through your own Gemini API key. The free tier is enough to play with. On the paid tier, a thinking cycle costs roughly one to two cents, which is a few dollars per hour of non-stop autonomy. When AGI mode is off, it costs nothing.

Is the software open source?

Yes. The firmware, the Python brain, the web dashboard and the full Blender and STL sources of the NOVA shell are all open. Hack it, extend it, teach it new skills.
